# FAQ — Frequently Asked Questions

---

## What is this project?

An open experimental investigation into whether quasi-periodic toroidal
winding geometries exhibit measurable electromagnetic behaviors different
from conventional periodic toroidal windings.

We are building physical prototypes, measuring them with real instruments,
and documenting everything openly so others can replicate or challenge the results.

---

**No.**

## Does this extract energy from the vacuum / zero point field?

**No claim of this kind is made.**

The project investigates whether winding geometry affects measurable
electromagnetic parameters — inductance, resonance, coupling, bandwidth.
That is the entirety of what is being tested.

## Are you claiming anomalous physics?

**No.**

The hypothesis is straightforward: winding geometry affects electromagnetic
behavior. This is already well-established in antenna theory, metamaterial
research, and resonator design. We are investigating whether it is also
meaningful in toroidal inductor topology.

If it is: interesting and potentially useful.
If it is not: also interesting, and the data will say so clearly.

---

## What does the golden ratio / Fibonacci / phi have to do with this?

The golden angle (137.5°) derived from the golden ratio is used as the
angular spacing between winding turns. This is a geometric design choice
with a specific mathematical property: irrational angular spacing means
no two turns ever occupy the same angular position, regardless of turn count.

This property — known as maximal irrationality — is why phyllotaxis
(sunflower seeds, pinecone scales) uses the same angle for optimal packing.

Whether this property produces useful electromagnetic effects in a toroidal
inductor is the experimental question. We do not assume the answer.

---

## Why toroids specifically?

Toroidal inductors are a well-characterized baseline. Their electromagnetic
behavior is well-understood, making it easier to identify genuine deviations
from expected behavior. They are also self-shielding, compact, and
widely used — making any findings potentially applicable.

---

## What measurement instruments are you using?

Primary measurement instrument: NanoVNA or equivalent vector network analyzer.
Supplementary: LCR meter, multimeter, oscilloscope.

All instruments are consumer-grade but calibrated and adequate for
the measurements being performed (impedance sweeps to 30 MHz,
inductance at audio frequencies, Q factor characterization).

---

## Can I replicate this?

Yes. That is the point.

Everything needed to replicate this project is in this repository:
- CAD files for the toroid former
- Build guides with complete materials lists
- Measurement protocols
- Blank measurement log templates
- Data analysis scripts

If you build one and measure it, please submit your data as a pull request
or open an issue. Independent replication is the most valuable contribution
you can make.

---

## Where is the data?

In the `/data/` directory. All raw measurement files (VNA Touchstone format,
CSV) are posted as they are collected. Nothing is withheld.

If the data shows nothing interesting, it will be posted anyway.
Null results are results.

## What do you expect to find?

Honestly: we don't know. That's why we're measuring.

The geometric reasoning suggests there might be differences in resonance
distribution and distributed capacitance. Adjacent research in aperiodic
EM structures (fractal antennas, quasi-crystal resonators) suggests
non-periodic geometry can matter. Whether it matters here, and how much,
will be determined by measurement.

---

## What if you find nothing?

Then the data clearly shows that conventional and quasi-periodic toroidal
windings behave identically within measurement precision.

That is a valid result. It would suggest that winding periodicity is not
a meaningful variable for this geometry and scale.

The data goes up either way.
